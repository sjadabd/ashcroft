Chapter I · 1881

A boy named
Arthur

Born in London, eighteen eighty-one. The city was still gas-lit. He would live long enough to dislike chalk, and to found a house that outlasted him.

Before the house existed

Dr. Shirley
Harcourt

A physician-chemist of the London School of Medicine for Women. They were married in 1907 at St George’s, Bloomsbury — before there was a shop, before there was a tin. The house began as a marriage. The formula came after.

Married 1907 · Then the work began

Autumn 1910

On a wet street
in Bloomsbury

They opened a narrow shop. Rain on the glass. The door opened at the same hour whether anyone came or not. Arthur was twenty-nine. Shirley kept the notes.

The chemist

Arthur Ashcroft

A dispensing chemist with an unreasonable dislike of chalk-based tooth powders. The powders of the day scoured more than they cleaned. He intended to remake them — and to put nothing in a tin he would not put in his own mouth.

1910 — 1914

Four winters

The powders of the day scoured more than they cleaned.

  • Grinding
  • Washing
  • Testing

Until he arrived at a soft paste of English peppermint and clove oil. He wrote it into the ledger as Formula No. 7.

A Tuesday

Ninepence

9d

He sold the first tin on a Tuesday for ninepence. Chalk-free. Soft. The shop’s customers began asking for the tin by its picture before they asked for it by name.

Formula No. 7 · Improved twice

Their daughter

Gladys
gave it a face

She grew up among the ledgers. When her hand was sure enough, she engraved the label herself, set the name in a hand she cut by eye, and pressed each tin with a fleur seal in red wax. Ornament, in this house, is earned — never bought in.

The house rule

Nothing goes in the tin that we would not put in our own mouths. Nothing goes on the tin that we did not draw ourselves.

Arthur & Shirley Ashcroft · from the first winter

Chapter II · 1924

From Edinburgh
to Bath

By nineteen twenty-four the fleur was on chemists’ shelves the length of the country. The tin travelled by its picture. The formula did not.

The second generation

Edward Harcourt
Ashcroft

Their son took the house in 1960. Spearmint became the first new flavour in fifty years. Navy and cream were fixed as house colours. The Blue Box was born. The formula was not rewritten. It was made quieter, and more sure of itself.

1960 · The Blue Box

Chapter III · 1999

DAMU Ltd.
takes the name

After the second generation, DAMU Ltd. of London acquired the brand name. Not a factory. The name, the ledgers, the original cuts. They did not put a tin on a shelf. First they went to work on themselves.

1999 — 2010

Eleven quiet
years

Laboratories. Archives. The identity redrawn from Gladys’s engravings — not replaced. Nano-hydroxyapatite. Clinical specialisation. They would not return the name to the shelf until the science was ready.

A century, then a return

2010 · The name
on the shelf again

A hundred years after Arthur opened the door, DAMU brought Ashcroft back — with modern science inside the old rule. Specialised pastes. A second century. The finest smile is still a British one.
